Curriculum & Structure

A curriculum with no two alike.

Six foundation areas, a spine of electives, and research seminars that grow into projects — 260 credits, entirely your own.

Start wide: six baskets, six credits each.

The rules that keep it rigorous

Flexible, not formless.

A few clear guardrails ensure freedom always adds up to a coherent, credible degree.

Max 30 core credits from a single department

At most five UG “core” (compulsory) courses from any one department — keeping the degree genuinely multidisciplinary. There is no cap on electives.

First year + CME = 260 credits

64 credits carried from the first year plus 196 across Semesters 3–8, all chosen in consultation with your Faculty Advisor.

Seminars grow into Projects

Four 4-credit research Seminars from Sem 3–6, followed by two 6-credit Projects in Sem 7–8, each with a guide of your choice and mid-term & end-term presentations.

Hands-on credit slots

LAB, SLP, PT and Work Visits contribute up to 18 credits and can replace any three elective slots — for students who learn by building.

Research

Seminars that become projects

From your third semester, research is woven into the degree. You choose a guide, dive deep into a domain, and present your work at mid-term and end-term reviews.

S3–6
FOUR SEMESTERS · 4 CREDITS EACH

Research Seminars

A literature-driven deep dive each semester, guided by a professor of your choice — building the skill of independent research.

S7–8
FINAL YEAR · 6 CREDITS EACH

Research Projects

Two full-scale projects that let you contribute original work — several CME papers have been accepted at ML and quantitative-finance conferences.

Minors are welcome. You can pursue a Minor from another department under standard institute rules — with no double-counting between Minor and Foundation courses, and from an area other than your concentration.

Availability of courses

High-demand electives are allocated centrally by CPI or lottery, just like every student. For advanced or departmental core courses you secure the instructor's consent — with guidance from your FacAd and the CME team.
